The practice / Philosophical ground / Reading guide 01

What remains
active?

Negromancy is a developing Black-Caribbean philosophy and practice investigating how historical relations continue to make worlds inhabitable, establish authority, form capacities and organize the possibilities of change.

Its starting question is not whether the past has disappeared. It is how relations formed through history act in the present, how we become able to recognize them, and what a different response might require.

01 / The governing problem

History does not end when an event ends.

A world is encountered through bodies, languages, institutions, technologies, rituals, inherited expectations and material conditions. Those mediations have histories.

Negromancy asks how their histories continue to organize perception and life even when the relations that produced them have been obscured, violently disrupted, naturalized or declared finished. This does not mean every present event has a single concealed origin. It means particular continuities, mechanisms and changes must be investigated.

Question 01

What relations are still acting here?

Identify historical relations active within a present encounter and the evidence that establishes them.

Question 02

How have we become capable of recognizing them?

Investigate mediation, embodiment, authority, education, listening and constraints on perception.

Question 03

What would have to change?

Distinguish recognizing a relation from rehearsing, sustaining and verifying a different response.

02 / The conceptual architecture

From historical relation to answerable transformation.

The manuscript is not one assertion repeated seventeen times. Its arguments move through different problems that have to be kept distinct.

01
Sections I–II

Relation and inhabitable worlds

A world is not merely a backdrop against which individuals act. Families, schools, states, plantations, markets and religious institutions organize relations that make certain ways of perceiving, belonging and acting available. These arrangements are historically produced, overlapping and incomplete.

Question of proof / practice. Study a particular institution rather than asserting that every relation works the same way. Which arrangements enable and constrain its participants?

02
Section III

Detonation, return and the Caribbean ground

The Caribbean is not an example chosen after the theory. Colonial conquest, enslavement, plantation production and racial classification are part of its intellectual ground. The manuscript proposes colonial-racial detonation for violent disarticulation and forced recomposition; returned relation for a relation that becomes active under changed conditions.

Question of proof / practice. Return is not restoration. Historical persistence must be shown through specific mechanisms, and African and Indigenous histories must not be represented as either completely erased or unchanged.

03
Sections IV–V

The embodied past; the emerging future

Historical consciousness is more than remembering events. Bodies acquire habits, skills, expectations, attachments and roles within historical arrangements. Casting names socially established expectations for a body; capture concerns the conditions that make another performance difficult to sustain.

Question of proof / practice. Recognition does not automatically provide agency. Rehearsal can disclose a possible response; material support may still be required to enact it.

04
Sections VI–VII

Mediation, authority and the limits of legibility

Languages, legal classifications, archives, rituals and technical systems help determine what becomes perceptible, recognizable, communicable and actionable. The manuscript asks who is authorized to mediate a relation and what happens when produced social arrangements appear self-sufficient. Fetish-space-time is a restricted, speculative diagnostic within this discussion.

Question of proof / practice. The legal designation of Obeah does not exhaust practitioners' relations. Neither does describing a classification prove its material effects; establish the mechanism in each case.

05
Sections VIII and XIII

Circulation, bearing and the difference repair makes

Institutions can appear coherent because their maintenance costs are borne unequally. Bearing investigates who carries labour, time, risk, care, injury, depleted capacity and future consequences. Conversion may reroute pressure without changing its distribution; repair requires a demonstrable change in the conditions through which harmful bearing recurs.

Question of proof / practice. An organization can become more efficient by externalizing costs. But not every redistribution repairs an injustice; examine whose burdens change, by how much, and over what time.

06
Section IX

Sacred relations, Mystery and opacity

Spiritual experience cannot simply be dismissed as cultural decoration, nor treated as automatic proof of supernatural causation. Mystery keeps an encounter open at the limits of explanation. Opacity sets limits upon another person's or institution's demand for exhaustive possession or transparency.

Question of proof / practice. Ask separately what was experienced, how a tradition interprets it, what practices accompanied it, what changed, and which causal claims can be supported.

07
Section X

The politics of witnessing and reception

An encounter can be witnessed, articulated and testified to without being received as knowledge. The auxiliary proposal Hardears Reason examines learned and institutional conditions of listening; rehearing names practices that might change them.

Question of proof / practice. The right to speak is not the same as the capacity to be heard. Yet rejection of a claim cannot be assumed to demonstrate bad faith; evidence and interpretation remain answerable.

08
Sections XI–XII

Repertoire, rehearsal and Conjuration

Dramatic rehearsal offers a practical model for experimenting with historically available responses. Repertoires may transmit obedience as readily as resistance. Conjuration makes historically active but obscured relations available for encounter through evidence, memory, performance and return; the wider public method remains recursive.

Question of proof / practice. The living may represent the dead, while historical institutions associated with the dead still shape the living. These are different processes, not proof of literal identical causation.

09
Sections XIV–XVII

Answerability, transformation and the human

Negromancy is philosophy and practice: archival research, performance, writing, contemplation, ritual, testimony and institutional analysis, with distinct evidentiary standards. Its reparative orientation asks what would change conditions, not merely descriptions. Prodigal Humanism emerges inside this programme as an unfinished inquiry into authorized genres of the human.

Question of proof / practice. A coherent interpretation is not evidence of its truth. The framework itself must be open to correction by the encounters it investigates.

For the full concept audit: the public concept ledger distinguishes active formulations from restricted and speculative proposals. In particular, fetish-space-time is not presented as an established general theory, and older methods are not silently reinstated.

03 / Scholars and works

What this philosophy reads—and what it does not inherit automatically.

These figures supply arguments that the manuscript develops in different directions; they are neither members of Morrow nor endorsers of Negromancy. The summaries below distinguish the works' contributions from the uses proposed by the manuscript. Where possible, the links lead to publishers or traceable primary texts.

01 / scholarly relation

Sylvia Wynter

Jamaican scholar of the human, coloniality and sociogeny

Work. “Unsettling the Coloniality of Being/Power/Truth/Freedom” (2003); “Towards the Sociogenic Principle” (2001)

Contribution. Wynter interrogates how a historically specific Western genre of Man is overrepresented as the human as such; her sociogenic inquiry makes history, meaning and institutional categories consequential to subject formation.

What the manuscript draws from it. Negromancy asks how historically produced classifications shape what appears natural, rational or fully human. The manuscript does not claim Wynter proves its entire philosophy.

02 / scholarly relation

Édouard Glissant

Martinican poet and philosopher of Relation and opacity

Work. Poétique de la Relation (1990); Poetics of Relation, Betsy Wing translation (1997)

Contribution. Glissant challenges the idea that beings can be understood as complete in isolation and defends the right not to be made fully transparent to another's demand for knowledge.

What the manuscript draws from it. The manuscript brings Relation into its conception of inhabitable worlds and opacity into its limits on total explanatory or administrative possession.

03 / scholarly relation

M. Jacqui Alexander

Caribbean feminist philosopher and theorist of sacred memory

Work. Pedagogies of Crossing: Meditations on Feminism, Sexual Politics, Memory, and the Sacred (2006 edition)

Contribution. Alexander investigates the conjunctions of colonial power, embodiment, political life, spiritual knowledge and practices of crossing, refusing easy separation of the sacred from historical existence.

What the manuscript draws from it. The manuscript treats sacred experience and memory as serious research fields while distinguishing participant interpretation from externally established causal claims.

Publisher ↗
04 / scholarly relation

Diana Taylor

Performance studies scholar

Work. The Archive and the Repertoire: Performing Cultural Memory in the Americas (2003)

Contribution. Taylor develops the repertoire of embodied practices as a mode of cultural memory and knowledge transmission, alongside and sometimes in tension with documentary archives.

What the manuscript draws from it. Negromancy uses this distinction to investigate what bodies transmit through repetition, and what might become possible through rehearsed alternative responses.

Publisher ↗
05 / scholarly relation

Joseph Roach

Scholar of circum-Atlantic performance and cultural memory

Work. Cities of the Dead: Circum-Atlantic Performance (1996)

Contribution. Roach follows remembrance, substitution and performance across Atlantic histories, showing how rituals and theatrical practices remake historical relations.

What the manuscript draws from it. The manuscript takes embodied transmission and the living/dead relation as questions for historically situated investigation, not as proof that every return operates identically.

Publisher ↗
06 / scholarly relation

Karl Marx

Political-economic genealogy, not a founding authority

Work. Capital, Volume I, chapter 1, section 4 (1867)

Contribution. Marx's analysis of commodity fetishism asks how social relations of production can appear as properties of commodities themselves.

What the manuscript draws from it. One precursor for the manuscript's fetish-space-time problem. The proposed extension to institutions, places and periods remains restricted and requires additional historical demonstration.

04 / Where the inquiry meets the world

An interpretation is not yet an intervention.

The theory's work is to make a historically situated claim examinable—and to ask what would have to change if the claim holds.

These are Morrow's research applications, not conclusions reached by the October manuscript. Each can refuse or correct the concepts brought to it.

Methodological test. A source may establish an event. An interpretation may connect it to another process. A policy recommendation introduces further values and consequences. A claim of repair needs evidence of changed conditions. Do not collapse these into a single assertion.

05 / Unresolved theoretical tensions

The inquiry must also investigate itself.

Section XVI of the manuscript explicitly identifies ten unresolved problems. They are not cosmetic reservations: they are potential points of failure, correction or transformation.

01 Relation and ontology

Does the inquiry concern historically mediated worlds, or the fundamental nature of all reality?

02 Historical constitution and agency

How do capacities emerge that exceed a learned or imposed role?

03 Mediation and material causality

When does a new classification change actual conditions, rather than only their description?

04 Opacity and knowledge

When is a refusal of total disclosure justified, and when does it conceal insufficient evidence?

05 Spirit and causation

How can sacred interpretations be taken seriously while assessing causal claims appropriately?

06 Bearing and repair

How should unavoidable costs and competing obligations be judged without simply moving burdens elsewhere?

07 Witness and authority

Who may legitimately represent a relation, and what makes testimony accountable?

08 Caribbean specificity and wider use

How can the concepts travel without erasing the historical ground that produced them?

09 Performance and explanation

What can embodied rehearsal investigate or transform, and what does it not explain?

10 Theory's own production

Can Negromancy expose and revise the histories, technologies and authorities through which it becomes credible?
