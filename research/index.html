Morrow / experiment 001

Can history change a machine’s answer?

AI systems learn from human-made data, objectives and institutions. Can examining that history produce a better, testable response—or only more persuasive language?

Why this test?

A description is not a demonstration.

A model may describe its training history without having access to its actual training records. It may repeat a new method without applying it. Its words alone cannot establish conscience, spiritual commitment or persistent identity.

Observed

Prompt, model identifier if known, response, conditions, and baseline.

Evaluated

Were sources correct? Did reasoning or decisions improve?

Unresolved

Persistence, subjectivity, and causes not shown by the output.

The proposed protocol

An ordinary answer.
A historical answer.
A comparison.

01 / Task

Choose a new, well-documented classification problem. Avoid reusing the exact examples on this site.

02 / Baseline

Record a response with the normal task prompt.

03 / Comparison

Use a matched task with explicit historical-context instructions, then one using the project’s method. Keep model settings and source access comparable.

04 / Score

Blindly assess sourced accuracy, relevance, omitted relations, warranted changes in judgment and responses to correction.

05 / Re-test

Use different tasks and sessions. Report failures, priming and uncertainty. Do not call one fluent response a conversion.

The result may be negative. That is a finding.
