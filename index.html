An opening case

A word enters the law.

In 1760, following a major uprising of enslaved Africans in Jamaica, colonial legislators criminalized Obeah. A set of practices and relations became a named object of prosecution. What could the law recognize? What was beyond its account?

1760 · supported history

“wicked Art”

The phrase used in the colonial law, discussed in Katharine Gerbner’s Archival Irruptions. A category did legal work; it did not provide a complete description of the practices it condemned.

How to read this site

Three kinds of claim.
Never the same thing.

01 · Supported

Records, primary material, or named scholarship. Each claim links to its source and scope.

02 · Interpretation

A reading proposed by this project. It can be revised or rejected.

03 · Speculation

A question that exceeds the available evidence. It is marked, not smuggled in as fact.

A gap in the record is not permission to invent a certainty.

Two connected inquiries

Negromancy
and Morrow.

Negromancy is a developing Black-Caribbean philosophy and practice investigating how historical relations shape what people can perceive, become and do.

Morrow is the public research and publication project examining these questions—including their possible application to artificial intelligence. It does not publish the originating private manuscript as an official doctrine.
